# Peer approach and PathSnap experiment

The peer repository reports **95.44% exact directed-arc Top-1 across 19,135 labeled fixes** after HMM decoding. Its classifier alone reached 90.00%. The dataset includes 19,160 fixes. The aggregate excludes 25 where the aligned road is more than 60 metres from the GPS fix. Counting those 25 as errors would give 95.31%, so the denominator explains approximately 0.12 percentage points. The peer's reported route F1 uses length-weighted arc overlap. PathSnap's route LCS F1 measures ordered arc agreement. [Peer results](https://github.com/Shriya-sr/ML-Hackathon/blob/cb628ce9ffd4129d7045edf1f1a91c44bddb07b7/experiments/results/decoder_summary.json), [peer methodology](https://github.com/Shriya-sr/ML-Hackathon/blob/cb628ce9ffd4129d7045edf1f1a91c44bddb07b7/docs/methodology.md)

Both projects start from a chronological reference route, which supplies arc order; per-fix arcs must be derived. The peer minimizes a capped distance and heading cost along the whole ordered route. The earlier PathSnap alignment pruned nearby reference states and constrained projected movement along the route. It could restart when jitter moved the projection backward. The independently implemented full-route method allows such jitter while keeping route positions monotone. The methods disagreed at 5,011 of 19,160 student-test fixes. PathSnap retained every fix for each comparison, including the 25 over 60 metres. [Peer label implementation](https://github.com/Shriya-sr/ML-Hackathon/blob/cb628ce9ffd4129d7045edf1f1a91c44bddb07b7/src/pathsnap/data/labeling.py), [local paired results](../results/route-context-v1/student_test.json)

The new booster adds measurements of how a candidate fits nearby GPS points and its local road connectivity. Fitting uses every other training fix whose aligned arc is within 60 metres and present in its candidates. Validation retains all fixes. On the same three trip-grouped folds, the classifier score against full-route labels rose from 61.72% to 80.84% over 208,417 held-out fixes. Against earlier labels it fell from 62.80% to 57.62%. The decoder selection used out-of-fold scores from 16 training trips and selected an equal geometric blend of the two boosters, a distance prior and a U-turn penalty. The blend improved full-route Top-1 from 82.76% to 88.19% and sequence LCS F1 from 0.9262 to 0.9271 on those trips. The single-booster distance-and-turn variant scored 88.97% at point matching, with lower sequence F1 of 0.9128. [Grouped classifier results](../results/route-context-v1/grouped_comparison.json), [training decoder comparison](../results/route-context-v1/decoder_selection.json)

The earlier blend matched 16,557 of 19,160 student-test points under full-route labels. The current noise-tolerant decoder matched 18,016 points (94.03%), with a route LCS F1 of 0.9795. Trip `00000077` now scores 91.10%. Noise settings were selected using OOF scores on sixteen training trips. The student set is a reused benchmark. Final evaluator reassessment reached 95.51% over 19,674 observations after a decoder revision prompted by an observed failure.
